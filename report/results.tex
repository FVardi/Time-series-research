\section{How this document works}

This document records every result of the project: the question a test was meant to
answer, how it was run, what came out, and what was concluded or decided from it.
Design decisions themselves live in \texttt{DECISIONS.md} and \texttt{decisions/}
(cited as D$n$); open questions for supervision in \texttt{QUESTIONS.md} (Q$n$).

\textbf{Entries.} Each result is an entry R$n$, numbered in the order the runs were made.
Numbers are never reused. An entry has: question, test, result, finding and consequence,
plus open points where something is unresolved.

\textbf{Status.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \current: the latest result for its question.
  \item \superseded{R$m$}: a later entry answers the same question, but this one is kept
        because it explains a decision or shows something the later one does not.
        Kept entries move to Appendix~\ref{app:history}.
  \item Superseded and replaced: a plain rerun with nothing to add. The text is removed;
        the entry number and its replacement stay in Table~\ref{tab:entries}.
\end{itemize}
Which status an entry gets is decided by the user, entry by entry.

\textbf{Numbers.} All tables, figures and numbers quoted in the text are generated by
\path{scripts/make_report.py} from the run folders (\texttt{runs/}, not committed);
Appendix~\ref{app:provenance} lists the folder behind each entry. Unless stated otherwise:
mean $\pm$ standard deviation over seeds (sample SD, $n-1$); the test set is scored once;
accuracies are fractions, not percentages.

\textbf{Reproduction criterion} (D26 item 6, also used in D38). Implemented in
\texttt{scripts/evaluate.py} and \texttt{scripts/tfc\_official.py} as
\[
  |\text{published} - \text{mean}| \;\le\; \max(2\,\mathrm{SD},\ 0.01).
\]

\section{Public benchmark: FordA}

FordA (UCR archive, used as distributed: D27, D30) is the first slice: a wrapped official
method, frozen representations, two probes (D26), three seeds, label budgets 1\,\%,
10\,\% and 100\,\% (D29, D40). Its purpose is to validate the harness against published
numbers before anything new is measured.

\entry{R3}{TS2Vec reproduces on FordA}{2026-10-08}{\current}
  {D26, D27, D28, D29}{\run{20261008_084722_ts2vec_forda}}

\question
Does TS2Vec, wrapped from its official code with the official settings, reproduce the
published FordA accuracy (0.936, batch size 8)?

\test
TS2Vec trained on the FordA training inputs (no labels) with the official defaults (D28;
600 iterations); one 320-dimensional representation per series, max-pooled over time.
Probes: TS2Vec's own RBF-SVM protocol and the harness logistic regression (D26), each at
three label budgets. Three seeds; GPU; committed code (commit \texttt{adf16c6}).

\result
SVM probe with all labels: \RthreeAcc{} (criterion: \RthreeVerdict, via the 1-pp clause;
Table~\ref{tab:repro}). All probes and budgets in Table~\ref{tab:forda} and
Figure~\ref{fig:forda}.

\finding
The wrapped TS2Vec and the evaluation pipeline reproduce the published number.

\consequence
The first slice is validated; a second method on the same data followed (R4, D32).

\entry{R4}{T-Loss reproduces on FordA; FordA does not separate the two methods}{2026-10-08}{\current}
  {D32 (D26, D27, D29)}{\run{20261008_092321_tloss_forda}}

\question
Does T-Loss, wrapped from its official code, reproduce its published FordA accuracy
(0.928, $K=10$)? And does FordA tell TS2Vec and T-Loss apart?

\test
As R3, with T-Loss's official settings (D32: 2,000 steps, $K = 10$, representation size
320). Same probes, label budgets and seeds. The working tree had uncommitted changes
(Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}).

\result
SVM probe with all labels: \RfourAcc{} (criterion: \RfourVerdict). At every probe and
label budget the two methods lie within about one standard deviation of each other
(Table~\ref{tab:forda}, Figure~\ref{fig:forda}).

\finding
T-Loss reproduces. On FordA the two methods are indistinguishable with three seeds.

\consequence
TF-C was not started on FordA: no published FordA number to validate it against, and
FordA does not separate the methods already wrapped (rationale of D35).

\section{\texorpdfstring{TF-C on FD-A $\rightarrow$ FD-B}{TF-C on FD-A to FD-B}}

TF-C is studied in steps (D35): (1) reproduce the published result with the official code;
(2) the official TF-C on the full, raw Paderborn data; (3) our own corrected TF-C;
(4) possibly a comparison on FordA. FD-A and FD-B are used as distributed by the TF-C
authors (D39): pretraining on FD-A (train), fine-tuning on the 60 FD-B training windows,
epoch selection on the \RsixNval{} FD-B validation windows, testing on FD-B test. Target:
$0.8934 \pm 0.0379$ accuracy, 5 runs (TF-C paper, Table 5; D38). The official reference is
the 2023 code (D36), run with two protocols: \emph{official} (epoch with the best
\emph{test} accuracy, as the official code) and \emph{clean} (epoch with the best
validation macro-F1, D37, D43).

\entry{R5}{Official TF-C diverges on the full FD-A data}{2026-10-08}{\current}
  {D36, D37, D38, D44}{\run{20261008_134951_tfc_official_fd}, \run{20261008_143021_tfc_official_fd},
  \run{20261008_155516_tfc_official_fd}}

\question
Does the official TF-C code, run on all FD-A windows (debugging subset off, D37),
reproduce the published FD-A $\to$ FD-B accuracy?

\test
Official 2023 code through our adapter, settings checked against the official
\path{FD_A_Configs.py} (40 epochs, batch 64, learning rate $3\cdot10^{-4}$, weight decay
$3\cdot10^{-4}$ as hard-coded). Five seeds. Three attempts: the first (seed 0 only) and second
with commit \texttt{87e9222}; the third with commit \texttt{c11957a}, which records a
non-finite pretraining loss as divergence and skips fine-tuning for that seed (D44).

\result
In \RfiveDiverged{} of \RfiveSeeds{} seeds the pretraining loss became non-finite
(Table~\ref{tab:tfc-official-full}, Figure~\ref{fig:tfc-loss}); in the first attempt
fine-tuning then crashed. The pretraining losses of the second and third attempt are
\RfiveIdentical{} for every seed, and the first attempt's finite losses (seed 0) are
\RfiveFirstIdentical{} to them, so the divergence is deterministic given the seed. Seed 4
completed pretraining, but no attempt wrote fine-tuning output for it, and the third
attempt wrote no \texttt{results.csv} (results file written: \RfiveResultsWritten).

\finding
On the full data, the official code does not produce a single test result. A likely
cause, not verified (D44): gradients are reset only once per epoch, so each step on the
full data uses many accumulated batches.

\consequence
D44: divergence is now recorded per seed as a result, and the official run was repeated
with the debugging subset (R9).

\openpoint{Why seed 4, whose pretraining finished, has no fine-tuning output, is not
recorded in the run folder.}

\entry{R6}{Our TF-C falls well below the published accuracy}{2026-10-08}{\current}
  {D41, D42 (D38, D39)}{\run{20261008_142751_tfc_fd}}

\question
Does our TF-C (paper's CNN and loss, corrected frequency augmentation, selection by
validation macro-F1; D41, D42) reach the published FD-A $\to$ FD-B accuracy?

\test
Settings in D41/D42 and the run's \texttt{config.yaml}. Five seeds. Component checks in
\texttt{tests/test\_tfc.py} (NT-Xent and $L_C$ equal the official functions; frequency
augmentation symmetric and the spectrum of a real signal; permutation reaches $M$
segments); their outcome is not stored with the run.

\result
Test accuracy \RsixAcc{}, macro-F1 \RsixFone{}; single seeds range from \RsixAccMin{} to
\RsixAccMax{} (Table~\ref{tab:tfc-ours}). The validation macro-F1 moves in large steps
between epochs (Figure~\ref{fig:tfc-val}); one validation window is $1/\RsixNval$ of the
validation set.

\finding
The mean is far below the published $0.8934$, with a large spread over seeds. Formally,
the criterion of D26 gives ``\RsixVerdict'', because twice the SD exceeds the gap
(Table~\ref{tab:repro}).

\consequence
D45 and D46: diagnostics to tell ``pretraining learned little'' from ``fine-tuning or
selection failed'' (R7) and to check whether FD-B can separate methods (R8).

\openpoint{The criterion passes a mean far below the target because the spread is large.
Is R6 a reproduction? Should the criterion get a condition on the spread (D26 item 6
speaks of the 1-pp clause only ``if the spread is very small'', while the code applies it
always)?}

\entry{R7}{Frozen probes on our TF-C encoders: pretraining is not where the shortfall is}{2026-10-08}{\current}
  {D45}{\run{20261008_151014_probe_tfc_fd}}

\question
Did our TF-C pretraining (R6) learn a representation that separates the FD-B classes, so
that the low fine-tuned accuracy comes from fine-tuning or epoch selection? Or did
pretraining learn little?

\test
The five pretrained encoders of R6 are frozen; their representation $[z_T; z_F]$ is probed
with both harness probes trained on all 60 FD-B training labels and scored once on FD-B
test (D45). Reference: untrained encoders of the same architecture, initialised with the
same seeds, probed the same way. 100\,\% labels only (exception to D40: 1\,\% of 60 is
less than one window).

\result
Pretrained encoder: SVM \RsevenPreSvm, logistic regression \RsevenPreLogreg.
Untrained encoder: SVM \RsevenRandSvm, logistic regression \RsevenRandLogreg{}
(Table~\ref{tab:fdb}, Figure~\ref{fig:fdb}).

\finding
Probes on the frozen pretrained encoders score far above the fine-tuned TF-C (R6), which
points to fine-tuning or epoch selection rather than pretraining. But pretraining does
not beat an untrained encoder with the SVM probe; it helps only the linear probe.

\entry{R8}{Plain spectra separate FD-B almost perfectly}{2026-10-08}{\current}
  {D46}{\run{20261008_162447_probe_spectrum_fd}}

\question
Can FD-B separate methods at all, given that an untrained encoder with an SVM probe
already reaches high accuracy (R7)?

\test
Both harness probes trained directly on the full FFT magnitude spectrum of each FD-B
training window (the input of TF-C's frequency branch), no encoder; 60 labels; scored once
on FD-B test. Deterministic, so one value per probe. Run in Claude's cloud workspace on
the work computer's FD-B data, copied unchanged (\texttt{NOTE.txt} in the run folder).

\result
SVM \ReightSvm, logistic regression \ReightLogreg{} (Table~\ref{tab:fdb}).

\finding
With 60 labels, an SVM on the plain spectrum classifies FD-B test almost without error,
above the published TF-C result. Under this protocol FD-B cannot show that a pretrained
method adds anything (the condition stated in D46's rationale is met).

\question
Is the debugging subset what makes the official code trainable, and does it then

\test

\finding
With the subset the official code trains without diverging, but the fine-tuned model
predicts a single class. Neither configuration of the official code reproduces the paper.

\section{Herning test rig}

The Herning run \run{scope_20260901_112732} is a validation run of an unfinished rig (D7,
D22): one bearing, one lubricant, rpm swept up and down at 13 temperature targets. The
regression target is $\kappa$, computed from rpm and temperature as in the MSSP code (D55).
Figure~\ref{fig:herning-op} shows the operating points.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{herning_operating_points}
\caption{Herning run: rpm (from the frequency-converter command), temperature and $\kappa$
for every sweep, in acquisition order, coloured by role in R10 (D52, D55).}
\label{fig:herning-op}
\end{figure}

\entry{R10}{Herning band check: where AE and US carry information}{2026-10-09}{\current}
  {D51, D52, D55; led to D60, D61, D62, Q3}{\run{20261009_085307_band_check_herning}}

\question
Before band-limiting and downsampling the rig signals (D51): in which frequency bands do
AE and US carry signal from the rotating bearing, and does that signal depend on the
operating point (D52)?

\test
\texttt{scripts/check\_bands.py} on the source file. Per sweep and sensor: Welch power
spectrum (Hann window, 65,536 samples, 50\,\% overlap; 38-Hz resolution at 2.5 MHz),
integrated over a low band (0--891 Hz) and base-10 third-octave bands from 1 kHz to
Nyquist. Sweeps: \RtenSweeps{} in total; \RtenAnalysed{} analysed (rotating, D55
exclusions applied), \RtenNoise{} noise reference (rpm target 100: bearing not rotating),
\RtenZero{} at rpm target 0, \RtenExcluded{} excluded (cool-down). Per band: ratio of the
median power of analysed sweeps to the noise reference (dB), and Spearman correlation of
band power with rpm, temperature and $\kappa$ ($\kappa$ from \RtenKappaMin{} to
\RtenKappaMax). Extra control against slow drift over the 13-hour run: the correlation with
rpm computed within each temperature block (median over blocks). Run in four chunks of
sweeps (3-minute limit per call), merged into one result (\texttt{NOTE.txt}). An earlier
folder (\run{20261009_085150_band_check_herning}) contains only the config.

\result
Figure~\ref{fig:bands}; all bands in Table~\ref{tab:bands} (Appendix~\ref{app:bands}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item AE: power above the reference from about 12.6 kHz to about 400 kHz, with peaks of
        \RtenAePeakLow{} and \RtenAePeakHigh. In the bands from 12.6 to 398 kHz the
        within-block correlation with rpm is \RtenAeCoreRpm. From 631 kHz up there is no
        excess power (at most \RtenAeTopDb~dB) and band power \emph{falls} with rpm within
        blocks ($\rho$ from \RtenAeTopRpm).
  \item US: \RtenUsLowDb~dB above the reference up to 6.3 kHz. Between about 40 and 200 kHz
        the excess is only \RtenUsMidDb~dB, but band power still rises with rpm within
        blocks ($\rho$ \RtenUsMidRpm) and correlates with $\kappa$ ($\rho$ \RtenUsMidKappa).
\end{itemize}

\finding
AE shows rpm-dependent power above the reference roughly between 12 and 560 kHz (the
origin of the part below 50 kHz is unknown, D60); above about 560 kHz it shows no excess
power, and the user regards it as noise (D61, D62). US carries most of its power below about 8 kHz, but rpm-dependent content continues
up to about 200 kHz. Correlation within temperature blocks rules out slow drift, but not
electrical interference that also scales with speed (Q3).

\consequence
Feature bands: AE 50--560 kHz (nothing below the 50-kHz coupler high-pass; D60, D61), US
0.71--178 kHz (D60). Downsampling: AE by 2 (1.25 MHz), US by 5 (500 kHz) (D62). Whether US
above about 40 kHz is bearing signal or interference is open (Q3).
