# Decisions — XJTU-SY bearing data

Dataset-specific decisions for the XJTU-SY run-to-failure bearing data (Xi'an Jiaotong University and Changxing Sumyoung Technology; https://github.com/WangBiaoXJTU/xjtu-sy-bearing-datasets). Index: `../../DECISIONS.md`. Adopted by D189.

## 2026-11-02 — D191: XJTU-SY tasks and derived labels
**Decision:** XJTU-SY is used for several tasks on the same frozen representations, each with the harness probe that fits it. The dataset provides run-to-failure recordings, not health labels; all labels below are derived in the pipeline from the recording times, and the stored data stay as recorded (D5).
1. *Fault element* (classification): the failed element per bearing, if the dataset's documentation lists it (to be confirmed at download).
2. *Remaining useful life* (regression, D58/D59 probe): piecewise-linear target — constant before the first prediction time (FPT), then decreasing linearly to 0 at the end of the run; normalised to [0, 1] per bearing. Metrics: the regression metrics of the harness (MAE, R²) plus RMSE.
3. *Health stage / detection* (classification, and the anomaly protocol of D192): two stages, healthy (before FPT) and degrading (after FPT).
4. *Health indicator* (label-free): the D192 anomaly score of each window against the bearing's own early-life windows, evaluated with monotonicity, trendability and prognosability (Coble & Hines, 2009; exact definitions to be taken from the source at implementation).
FPT (one definition for tasks 2–4): the 3σ rule on the root-mean-square value — FPT is the first time the RMS exceeds the mean + 3 standard deviations of an early-life reference segment. Open, settled after looking at the data: the reference segment length, the channel(s) used, and how many consecutive exceedances are required (guard against single spikes).
Split by bearing (D121); conditions and number of held-out bearings decided with the configuration.
**Alternatives considered:** Linear remaining-useful-life target from the start of the run (penalises healthy early life). More than two health stages (no common definition). FPT by fixed fractions of life (ignores the large spread of bearing lives).
**Rationale:** User delegated the choice to Claude (2026-11-02), asking for the most widely used and/or most flexible options. The piecewise-linear target with an FPT from a 3σ rule is a common choice in bearing prognostics; one FPT definition keeps the tasks consistent; all labels are derived, so they can be changed without reconverting.

*Note (2026-11-05): task 1 (fault element) is dropped and the split settled by D231 (DSCN protocol); tasks 3 and 4 are evaluated on 1_5 and 2_5.*

*Note (2026-11-05): the target of task 2 is superseded by D228 (linear, normalised by lifetime, no FPT); the FPT channel is settled by D229 (horizontal); FPT is now used by tasks 3 and 4 only.*

*Note (2026-11-03): task 1 confirmed. Table 2 of `Introduction_to_XJTU-SY_Bearing_Dataset.pdf` (in the archive) lists the fault element per bearing: outer race 8 (1_1, 1_2, 1_3, 2_2, 2_4, 2_5, 3_1, 3_5), inner race 3 (2_1, 3_3, 3_4), cage 2 (1_4, 2_3), combined 2 (1_5: inner and outer race; 3_2: inner race, ball, cage and outer race). Classes are small and unbalanced for a split by bearing, and the label describes the end of life, not the healthy start; both to be handled in the task configuration. Bearing type LDK UER204; test ended when the vibration amplitude exceeded 10 × the normal-stage maximum.*

## 2026-11-05 — D224: XJTU-SY conversion: dataset documentation, otherwise harness conventions
**Decision:** Where the dataset's documentation and the literature using it have a standard, it is followed; otherwise the harness's conventions (D1–D6) and the usual time-series practice. Sources: `Introduction_to_XJTU-SY_Bearing_Dataset.pdf` (in the dataset folder; "intro") and Lei et al. (2019), J. Mech. Eng. 55(16), doi 10.3901/JME.2019.16.001 (in `Important References/`).
1. *Signals:* both accelerometer channels, horizontal and vertical, 25.6 kHz (intro Section 2.4); channel names as in the CSV header, `Horizontal_vibration_signals` and `Vertical_vibration_signals`.
2. *Hierarchy:* one run per bearing (one run-to-failure test; 15 runs, run ID e.g. `Bearing1_1`); each CSV (one 1.28 s sample of 32,768 points) one capture, `m0001`…`m2538` (file number, zero-padded); one stream (the two accelerometers share a clock).
3. *Timing:* capture start time nominal, (n − 1) × 60 s, with `timing_accuracy` "nominal: 1 min sampling period from the documentation; no timestamps in the files". Files are named in sampling order (Lei et al., 2019, Section 1.3); the files carry no timestamps, and their modification times (2019-01-31, about 20 s per bearing) are export times, not used.
4. *Operating condition:* nominal speed (2,100 / 2,250 / 2,400 rpm), shaft frequency (35 / 37.5 / 40 Hz) and radial force (12 / 11 / 10 kN) per capture in the telemetry table (intro Section 2.3).
5. *Per-capture columns (D31):* bearing, condition (folder name, e.g. `35Hz12kN`), file number, fault element verbatim from intro Table 2 (class grouping left to the task configuration, D191), `identical_to`. No derived labels; first prediction time, remaining useful life and health stages are derived in the pipeline (D191).
6. *Run extras:* documented lifetime (intro Table 2), L10 range (Lei et al., 2019, Table 3), bearing type LDK UER204 and its geometry (intro Table 1), sensors (2 × PCB 352C33 on magnetic mounts at 90°, DT9837 acquisition unit; Lei et al., 2019, Section 1.2), stop criterion (10 × A_h, intro Section 3).
7. *Units:* `g`. Not stated in the files or the intro's text; Lei et al. (2019) Section 3.2 gives amplitudes "up to 50 g (g = gravitational acceleration)" and the intro's Fig. 4 axes read "Amplitude (g)"; the largest value in the data is 54.4. The source is recorded in the run's extras.
8. *Status and type:* `final` (public benchmark); 64-bit floats as in the files (scale 1, offset 0; D6): the CSVs hold 17 significant digits, values are not on an exact integer grid and not exactly representable in float32. Parsed with pandas 3's default parser, which reads them back exactly (checked against Python's `float()` in `tests/test_xjtu_sy.py`). Source identification per D24 as for Paderborn: file name pattern, total size, latest modification time.
9. *Quirks:* recorded, not fixed: gaps in the file numbering, file counts differing from intro Table 2, unusual sample counts, NaN values, exact copies within a bearing; an unexpected header or column count stops the conversion. A read-only scan of all 9,216 files before conversion (2026-11-05) found none: one header throughout, 32,768 × 2 values per file, no NaNs, no duplicate files, numbering 1…N without gaps, counts as in Table 2.
**Alternatives considered:** Short channel names (`horizontal`, `vertical`). One run per CSV (9,216 runs) or per condition (3 runs). No start time (file number only). Units `unknown` (as Paderborn, where nothing is stated).
**Rationale:** User's decision (2026-11-05): approved as proposed; stay as close as possible to the literature standard for this dataset, otherwise to general time-series practice. A run is one test and a capture one uninterrupted acquisition (D1), matching how the dataset is documented and used (each bearing a run-to-failure sequence of one-minute samples). The nominal start time is what the remaining-useful-life labels of D191 need.

*Note (2026-11-05): item 8's parser was wrong on the project environment. pandas 3.0.6 (the version in `uv.lock`) does not read the 17-digit values exactly with its default parser (differences of up to 6,332 ulp, relative error below 1e-12); the check had been done with pandas 3.1.1, where it is exact. The first conversion was therefore not "as recorded" (D5) and was deleted; the reader now parses with Python's `float()` (correctly rounded on every platform, independent of the pandas version) and the data were reconverted. Found by the full comparison of all 9,216 files after conversion.*

## 2026-11-05 — D228: XJTU-SY remaining-useful-life target: linear, normalised by lifetime, as DSCN (supersedes the target of D191 task 2)
**Decision:** The remaining-useful-life target follows the dataset authors' deep-learning protocol, DSCN (Wang, Lei, Li, Yan, 2019, Mech. Syst. Signal Process. 134, 106330, Section 4.1.3; in `Important References/`): no first prediction time; the actual RUL of each bearing is divided by its lifetime, giving a target in [0, 1] that decreases linearly from the start of the run to 0 at the end. Inferred, not stated in the paper, and marked as such: the inspection time of file n is n minutes and the lifetime is the number of files N in minutes (intro Table 2 gives lifetimes equal to the file counts), so the target of file n is (N − n) / N; this is consistent with DSCN's Eq. 15, which converts a prediction back to minutes as RUL = NormRUL · P_t / (1 − NormRUL). The target is derived in the pipeline (D5, D191); the stored data are unchanged.
Other parts of the DSCN protocol, recorded for the later decisions and not decided here: split per condition, the first four bearings for training and the fifth (1_5, 2_5, 3_5) for testing (Table 1); both channels as input, each sample z-scored by its own mean and standard deviation (Eq. 14); time-window embedding of the current and S − 1 previous samples (Eq. 16); metrics: the PHM 2008 scoring function (Eq. 12) and RMSE (Eq. 13) on RUL in minutes, computed "from the half to the end of lifetime" of each test bearing; 20 repetitions. Quirk: DSCN gives the lifetimes as "from 33 min to 2538 min"; the shortest bearing in the data (Bearing2_4) has 42 files (intro Table 2: 42 min).
**Alternatives considered:** D191's piecewise-linear target (constant before FPT, linear after; used with the FPT method of Li et al., 2015, in Wang et al., 2020); reporting both targets.
**Rationale:** User's decision (2026-11-05): DSCN. Both targets come from the dataset's authors; the DSCN target needs no FPT, which is ambiguous for Bearing1_4, Bearing3_2 and Bearing3_5 (RMS check, 2026-11-05).

## 2026-11-05 — D229: XJTU-SY first prediction time on the horizontal channel (settles one open point of D191)
**Decision:** The first prediction time (FPT; still needed for D191's health-stage and health-indicator tasks 3 and 4) is computed from the horizontal vibration channel only, as by the dataset authors: Wang et al. (2020, IEEE Trans. Reliab. 69(1), case study on the XJTU-SY data: "because the load is applied in the horizontal direction, the accelerometer placed in this direction is able to capture more degradation information … the horizontal vibration signals are selected") and Lei et al. (2019, Section 2.2, who choose the horizontal channel for their spectrum analysis for the same reason). The model inputs are not affected (both channels are stored and can be used, D224). Still open: the reference segment and the number of consecutive exceedances; to be taken from the dataset authors' FPT method, Li, Lei, Lin, Ding (2015), "An improved exponential model for predicting remaining useful life of rolling element bearings", IEEE Trans. Ind. Electron. 62(12), 7762–7773, doi 10.1109/TIE.2015.2455055 (cited by Wang et al., 2020, for the start of degradation; full text not yet available).
**Alternatives considered:** Both channels (FPT at the first crossing on either); vertical only. With a 30-minute reference and 3 consecutive exceedances the two channels agree on 11 bearings and differ on 2_5, 3_1, 3_2, 3_4 and 3_5 (RMS check, 2026-11-05); "either channel" moves FPT earlier, on Bearing3_4 to a temporary rise near minute 1,000 instead of the onset at about 1,418.
**Rationale:** User's decision (2026-11-05): the standard for this dataset.

## 2026-11-05 — D230: XJTU-SY first prediction time: Li et al. (2015) as published, 20-minute reference; four bearings left out of tasks 3 and 4 (settles D191's remaining FPT points)
**Decision:** FPT (used by D191's health-stage and health-indicator tasks 3 and 4; not by the RUL target, D228) follows the dataset authors' method, Li, Lei, Lin, Ding (2015), IEEE Trans. Ind. Electron. 62(12), 7762–7773, doi 10.1109/TIE.2015.2455055, Section III (cited for the start of degradation by Wang et al., 2020), on the horizontal channel (D229):
1. *Feature:* kurtosis of each one-minute sample (Li et al.: kurtosis as the monitoring index, RMS only for prediction). Computed as the fourth central moment over the squared variance (non-excess; about 3 for a healthy bearing, as in Li et al.'s Figs. 5 and 12).
2. *Interval:* two-sided, [μ − 3σ, μ + 3σ], μ and σ the mean and standard deviation of the kurtosis over a fixed normal-operation reference segment (fixed: Li et al.'s figures draw the interval as constant lines over the whole life).
3. *Trigger:* FPT₀ = the first value outside the interval; for l = 1, 2, …, FPT_l = the first start of l + 1 consecutive values outside it; stop when FPT_l = FPT_(l−1), which is the FPT; no FPT if no such run exists (Li et al.'s "N/A").
4. *Our choices, not in the source (marked as such):* the reference segment is the first 20 minutes of each bearing (Li et al. give no length; 10 minutes gave early false triggers on 1_1, 3_2, 3_5; 20 and 30 minutes agree except on 1_1 and 1_4, where 20 is closer to the visible onset); the search for exceedances starts after the reference segment (Li et al. compare each new value with the interval); sample standard deviation (ddof = 1).
5. *Bearings left out of tasks 3 and 4:* Bearing2_4 and Bearing3_5 (no FPT), Bearing3_4 (FPT at minute 52, inside the start-up transient of the kurtosis, about 1,370 minutes before the RMS onset), Bearing3_2 (no clear onset in kurtosis or RMS). They remain in the RUL (D228) and fault-element tasks.
Resulting FPT (minute, check of 2026-11-05 on the converted data): 1_1 76, 1_2 44, 1_3 60, 1_4 80, 1_5 36, 2_1 455, 2_2 48, 2_3 128, 2_5 142, 3_1 2410, 3_3 340.
**Alternatives considered:** Reference segments of 10 or 30 minutes; keeping the method's output for all bearings; departing from Li et al. (e.g. skipping a start-up period, RMS instead of kurtosis, one-sided interval). No published per-bearing FPT values exist to check against (Wang et al., 2020, analyse 1_1, 2_1, 3_1 without stating them).
**Rationale:** User's decision (2026-11-05), following Claude's suggestions: the published rule unchanged where the source is explicit; the bearings where it demonstrably fails are left out of the two tasks rather than labelled wrongly.

## 2026-11-05 — D231: XJTU-SY split: DSCN protocol (bearings 1–4 of each condition train, bearing 5 test); pooled probe; DSCN metrics; fault-element task dropped (settles D191's split; supersedes D191 task 1)
**Decision:** One split (D214), the dataset authors' protocol from DSCN (Wang, Lei, Li, Yan, 2019, Mech. Syst. Signal Process. 134, 106330, Section 4.1.1, Table 1):
1. *Split:* in each operating condition, Bearings 1–4 for training and Bearing 5 for testing: training 1_1–1_4, 2_1–2_4, 3_1–3_4; test 1_5, 2_5, 3_5.
2. *One probe pooled over the three conditions* (12 training bearings, 3 test bearings). DSCN does not state whether it trains one model or one per condition; pooling is our choice, marked as such.
3. *Pretraining* (and any other fitting without labels) uses every recording except the three test bearings (D121; as D221 for Paderborn).
4. *Metrics (RUL, D228):* as DSCN, the PHM 2008 scoring function (DSCN Eq. 12: d_i = PreRUL_i − ActRUL_i; e^(−d_i/13) − 1 for d_i < 0, e^(d_i/10) − 1 for d_i ≥ 0, summed over the test samples) and RMSE (Eq. 13), on RUL in minutes (predictions converted back with Eq. 15), computed "from the half to the end of lifetime" of each test bearing. In addition, the harness's MAE and R² on the same span (D191's metrics; an addition beyond DSCN).
5. *Fault-element task (D191 task 1) dropped for XJTU-SY:* under this split the test bearings are inner + outer race (1_5), outer race (2_5) and outer race (3_5), so inner race and cage are never tested and the combined class is never trained; a second split for it is ruled out by D214. The fault element stays in the captures table (D224).
6. *Health-stage and health-indicator tasks (D191 tasks 3 and 4)* are evaluated on the test bearings 1_5 and 2_5 only (3_5 is left out by D230).
Open (next step): windows and input. DSCN feeds each whole one-minute sample, both channels, each z-scored by its own mean and standard deviation (Eq. 14), with the S − 1 previous samples (time-window embedding, Eq. 16). Repetitions (DSCN: 20) follow the harness's seed policy.
**Alternatives considered:** Leave one bearing out per condition (15 folds; used as the benchmark in the GNN RUL survey, arXiv 2409.19629, Section 4.1). One probe per condition (four training bearings each). Only DSCN's metrics; only the harness's. Keeping the fault-element task with another split. Dropping tasks 3 and 4 as well.
**Rationale:** User's decision (2026-11-05), approving Claude's proposal: DSCN is the dataset authors' protocol, consistent with the RUL target (D228), and a single split (D214). No XJTU-SY split is described as standard in the literature checked (the survey above; arXiv 2607.28115, which uses the same Bearing 5 hold-out).

## 2026-11-05 — D232: XJTU-SY windows, input and task configuration (DSCN where it is explicit)
**Decision:** Following the DSCN protocol (Wang, Lei, Li, Yan, 2019; D228, D231) where it is explicit; our choices are marked.
1. *Windows:* one window per one-minute sample, the whole 32,768 points at 25.6 kHz (DSCN: the data "acquired at each sampling time step"); no overlap, no resampling.
2. *Channels:* both, horizontal and vertical (DSCN Section 4.1.1).
3. *Normalisation:* each window and channel z-scored with its own mean and standard deviation (DSCN Eq. 14), for pretraining and probe inputs alike; config `standardise: per_window`. This replaces D137's pretraining statistics for XJTU-SY. As in DSCN, the absolute amplitude is removed from the inputs.
4. *Time-window embedding for the RUL task* (DSCN Eq. 16, S = 5 as chosen in its Section 4.1.5): the probe input of minute n is [z_(n−4); …; z_n], the frozen representations of the same bearing concatenated in time order. Our adaptation: DSCN embeds the raw inputs into its network, whereas a frozen encoder sees single windows. Minutes 1–4 of each bearing (no full history) are left out of probe training and test; they lie outside the evaluation span of every test bearing (item 6). Not used for the health-stage task (no protocol for it).
5. *RUL probe:* ridge, alpha by 4-fold cross-validation on folds grouped by bearing, shuffled with the run's seed (4 folds as DSCN's hyperparameter selection, Section 4.1.4; grouped by bearing as D129); floor: floor_mean; label fractions 0.01, 0.1, 1.0, drawn unstratified (D170) over the embedded minutes.
6. *RUL metrics, details of D231 item 4:* the predicted normalised RUL is clipped to [0, 0.99] before DSCN's Eq. 15 converts it to minutes (lower bound: DSCN's ReLU output layer, Section 3; upper bound our choice, because Eq. 15 is undefined at 1); the number of clipped predictions is reported. Evaluation span: minutes n ≥ N/2 of each test bearing (N = its lifetime in minutes, our reading of "from the half to the end of lifetime"). Per test bearing: the score (sum over the span), RMSE in minutes, and MAE and R² of the normalised target over the same span; the summary is the mean over the three test bearings; per-bearing values in per_bearing.csv.
7. *Health-stage task (D191 task 3):* label "healthy" for minutes n < FPT and "degrading" for n ≥ FPT (FPT from D230). Training bearings 1_1–1_4, 2_1–2_3, 3_1, 3_3; test 1_5 and 2_5; 2_4, 3_2 and 3_4 are used in pretraining only (D230, D231 items 3 and 6). Probes: logreg with folds grouped by bearing (D129) and floor; metrics: accuracy and macro-F1 (D26). Windows and normalisation as items 1–3.
8. *Health-indicator task (D191 task 4):* waits for the anomaly scorers of D192 (not implemented).
9. *FPT in the harness:* the D230 values are a table in `harness/fpt.py`, next to the function that computes them (Li et al., 2015); a test recomputes the table from the converted data.
10. *Configs:* TF-C positives (D123) for the RUL and health-stage tasks; other methods are copies with their own settings.
**Alternatives considered:** Shorter windows cut from each minute (several windows per minute; not DSCN). D137's pretraining statistics (keeps amplitude; not DSCN). No time-window embedding (S = 1, also tested in DSCN). Ridge folds not grouped (D170) or 5 grouped folds (D129). Evaluation over the whole life.
**Rationale:** User delegated the choices to Claude (2026-11-05: finish with Claude's recommendations, adhering to the decisions and matching the literature standard).
